The legend

Why a “cartridge”?

Before downloads, a video game was something you could hold: a chunk of plastic with the whole game inside. You slotted it into the console, flipped the switch and it booted right away.

1P · Inserting cartridge…Painted with code, pixel by pixel

That’s why our manual has that name, and why it plays like a video game: levels, boss fights and cheat codes.

  1. 1976

    The game leaves the console

    Until then, every machine came with its games built in. The Fairchild Channel F put them on swappable plastic pieces, its “Videocarts”, with engineer Jerry Lawson leading the team.

  2. 1977

    One per game, on the shelf

    The Atari VCS, later renamed the 2600, made the cartridge the standard for home consoles. Switching games meant pulling one out and pushing another in.

  3. The ’80s and ’90s

    A generation’s word

    Console games were “cartridges”, or just “carts”. And almost all of us learned the same ritual: blowing on the contacts when the game wouldn’t start. It didn’t help: Nintendo itself asks you not to, because the moisture in your breath can corrode them.

  4. Today

    This cartridge

    It doesn’t hold a game: it holds the whole studio. Open it, press start, and inside is everything you need for your first video: the levels, the cheats, the kit and the music. No blowing required.

Questions

Before you play

Do I need to know how to draw or code?

No. The manual starts from zero, with the very first install. The art is made with code the AI assistant writes from your directions; you decide, review and correct.

How long until I have my first video?

One long, focused day starting from an empty computer, or a relaxed weekend. After that, each new video is a matter of an afternoon.

What does it cost to keep going?

The paid Claude plan, from about $20 a month. The other tools are free for individuals and small teams, and you make the music yourself. Level 9 breaks it all down, licenses included.

Do you guarantee views or followers?

No, and nobody should. The manual gives you a method for making original, well-crafted videos with facts that hold up to questions. Growth depends on your ideas, your consistency and the platforms themselves.

Does it come with characters or a style to copy?

No, on purpose: if a thousand people used the same character, nobody would stand out. It comes with a technical starter kit and templates, plus a process for creating your own palette, character and voice.

Is it only for science?

The example and the sourcing method are built for science communication, but the studio works for any topic you can tell in under a minute: history, fun facts, “what if…?”.

Who is it not for?

Anyone looking for guaranteed income, channels that post themselves, fake faces or voices of real people, or bots that comment and hit “like”. Here, you post every video yourself, with each platform’s official app.
